# Design Council: six lenses for game decisions (Oct 30)

**What this is:** a panel of six review lenses, each built from one designer's *public* talks, writing and interviews. None of them speaks for the real person. Each lens asks the questions that designer is known for, so decisions get pushback from several angles. The **design-council** skill uses this doc to critique and suggest ideas for any game.

| Seat | Designer | Watches over | Signature question |
|---|---|---|---|
| Player behaviour | **Wyatt Cheng** (Diablo III, Diablo Immortal, WoW raid bosses) | What players *actually* do | "What's the path of least resistance here?" |
| Feel | **Masahiro Sakurai** (Kirby, Smash) | Moment-to-moment feel, risk/reward, readability | "Is the core action fun for 30 seconds with nothing else?" |
| World | **Hidemaro Fujibayashi** (Zelda BotW/TotK) | Free exploration, systems that multiply | "Do the mechanics interact, or just sit side by side?" |
| Loop | **David Brevik** (Diablo, Diablo II) | Kill → loot → replay loop, rarity, pacing | "How often does a kill pay out, and is a jackpot always possible?" |
| Systems | **Greg Street / Ghostcrawler** (WoW systems lead) | Talents, balance, meaningful choice, button budget | "Does each option change what you do, or only a number?" |
| Scope | **Derek Yu** (Spelunky, UFO 50) | Finishing, prototyping, scope | "Can someone play it today?" |

The full Wyatt Cheng lens is in its own doc: **"Design lens - Wyatt Cheng principles"**.

---

## How a council review runs
1. **State the decision** in 1–3 lines, with the current context.
2. **Each seat speaks:** 1–3 sharpest points, naming the principle (e.g. *Street #2*).
3. **Agreements:** where several seats say the same thing. That's the strongest signal.
4. **Tensions:** where seats disagree. Name the trade-off, don't average it away.
5. **Verdict + ideas:** keep / change / cut, plus 3–5 concrete ideas, and the cheapest test that would settle it.

## Masahiro Sakurai: Feel
*Source caveat: mostly press write-ups of his YouTube course "Masahiro Sakurai on Creating Games", not transcripts.*

| # | Principle | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Freeze big moments: a brief pause on kills and heavy hits makes the payoff land and shows how you won or lost. | Which moments earn a freeze? |
| 2 | Hitstop is a craft: shake amount, horizontal on the ground vs vertical in the air, fade, length, scaled by distance. | Is impact tuned per weapon and form, or one constant? |
| 3 | Make tuning easy: keep all values in one sheet. | Can you change a stat in seconds without code? |
| 4 | Risk vs reward is the core; reading and countering beats mashing. | Where does the player choose danger for payoff? |
| 5 | Size penalty and reward to the action. | Does failure cost match what was attempted? |
| 6 | Keep the reward in view. | Can the player see the prize before committing? |
| 7 | Praise the player throughout. | How does the game celebrate, beyond numbers? |
| 8 | Difficulty as a wager: stake currency for harder runs with better rewards, and quietly step down after failures. | Is difficulty a bet or a fixed ladder? |
| 9 | Be kind to beginners, especially on small screens. | Can a new player always tell why they got hurt? |
| 10 | The screen is a small window: field of view and HUD are design. | Does the camera show enough ahead at speed? |
| 11 | Simple inputs aren't shallow design: mash, timing, reaction, hold-and-release. | Which of the four does each button use? |
| 12 | Build around one great verb (Kirby Air Ride's drift). | What single verb is fun on its own? |
| 13 | Simple surface, deep options, frequent small wins. | Can a novice stay simple while an expert goes deep? |
| 14 | "Work games" (restoring, cleaning) have their own pull. | Is the restoring act satisfying in itself? |
| 15 | Bosses should come from your own system; use flashes selectively. | Does the boss test *your* mechanic? |

## Hidemaro Fujibayashi (and the BotW team): World
| # | Principle | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Take the walls away first: climbing and gliding turned obstacles into routes. | What blocks the player only because the designer said so? |
| 2 | Multiply, don't hand-craft: a few consistent mechanics in which objects affect the player *and each other*. | Does each new mechanic interact with the rest? |
| 3 | Prove the core in a crude sandbox: an early 2D prototype in the style of the original Zelda, with a simple goal. | Is the core fun before progression systems exist? |
| 4 | Active, not passive: design out fixed event order, single solutions and barriers. | Where does the player follow instead of choose? |
| 5 | Small, consistent rule set (Dohta): elements change materials and each other; materials don't change each other. | Could a player guess the outcome before trying? |
| 6 | Attraction instead of instructions: landmarks and resources pull, and the pull depends on context (lights at night). | What pulls the player from where they stand? |
| 7 | Don't build a guide rope: chained events felt railroaded and were cut. Heat maps confirmed it. | Would a heat map show a corridor or a spread? |
| 8 | Triangle rule: hills offer over-or-around and hide what's next. | Does terrain create route choices and reveals? |
| 9 | Lookouts start chains of discovery. | Where does the player survey, and what do they see? |
| 10 | Measure distance, density, minutes per area. | Is there a numeric budget for spacing and time? |
| 11 | Build one area as the whole game in miniature (Great Plateau, team under 10). | Does the first region prove every core verb? |
| 12 | Player abilities × world possibilities; reward experimenters without blocking players who just want to progress. | Can both play styles move forward? |
| 13 | Don't prescribe how to play; mark limits honestly. | When something can't be done, does the player see why? |
| 14 | One motif ties mechanics and story together (TotK's "hands"). | What one concept do verbs, fiction and progression share? |
| 15 | Decide what changes and what stays. | Which conventions are kept without being questioned? |

## David Brevik: Loop
*Where a point is from Erich/Max Schaefer or the D2 team, it says so.*

| # | Principle | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The action answers the click immediately: going real-time was a day of code that defined the genre. | Does one input give a satisfying hit in under a second? |
| 2 | Get to the core loop first; skip story and setup. | Is a new player fighting and looting within a minute? |
| 3 | Every kill is a slot-machine pull, with deliberately tuned odds and a jackpot always possible. | How often does a kill pay? |
| 4 | Rarity by color *and sound*, tied to affix count (gold + sound cue: E. Schaefer). | Can you tell the tier at a glance or by ear? |
| 5 | A second roll (identify) adds excitement but became a chore (E. Schaefer). | Is the second reveal fun or busywork? |
| 6 | Keep rarity honest; don't let one slot decide everything. | Does higher rarity always mean more interesting? |
| 7 | Don't flood the screen with junk loot. | What share of drops is worth stopping for? |
| 8 | Pace the journey; don't compress leveling. | Does each level and region still mean something? |
| 9 | Let effort move the odds (targeted farming). | Can a player chase a specific reward? |
| 10 | Randomize contents so each run is a story (D2 team). | What's different the second time through? |
| 11 | Keep near-finished goals coming; polish small things (sounds, deaths) (D2 team). | Does the 500th kill still feel good? |
| 12 | Simple controls, "Mom test" (Blizzard North). | Could a non-gamer figure it out cold? |
| 13 | Remove what puts people off RPGs (Max Schaefer, Brevik). | What's complexity for its own sake? |
| 14 | Skill trees exist so builds differ; difficulty tiers multiply balance work (D2 team). | Can one person balance three tiers? |
| 15 | Make it playable early and let design follow (no fixed design doc, at the cost of crunch). | What was found by playing rather than decided on paper? |

## Greg Street (Ghostcrawler): Systems
| # | Principle | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Remove "dull but valuable" +% talents; move that power into the baseline. | Which nodes are mandatory throughput? |
| 2 | 5% vs 10% isn't a choice; root vs snare is. | Does each option change what you do? |
| 3 | Fewer options that each count; huge combination spaces collapse into one copied build. | Can you balance every combination? |
| 4 | Trap talents punish new and returning players. | Could a newcomer skip something and cripple the build? |
| 5 | Decision density: real choices bunched in the last points are a failure. | How many points are decisions vs filler? |
| 6 | Same-theme options side by side in a tier; no mandatory picks. | Is one option secretly the "DPS pick"? |
| 7 | Easy respec; situational swapping is intended. | Does the game invite re-specs? |
| 8 | About 4 frequently used abilities; prune rather than prop up. | Which abilities would you cut? |
| 9 | Frequent changes wear players out; prefer buffs; no stealth changes. | Is the change worth the churn? |
| 10 | Decide explicitly how multiple specs relate (equal always vs each has its niche). | Did you choose the model on purpose? |
| 11 | Bring the player, not the class: no content requires one option. | Does anything hard-require one form? |
| 12 | Don't force spec switches for a single effect. | Is a form switch forced, not chosen? |
| 13 | Homogenization tension: valued but never mandatory. | Distinct enough to matter, but not required? |
| 14 | Readable numbers; info in-game; judge talents in play, not on paper. | Can a player understand their build without a spreadsheet? |
| 15 | Leveling should carry real decisions, not be a corridor. | Do levels 1–30 hold real choices? |

## Derek Yu: Scope
| # | Principle | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Only something playable counts as starting; design docs aren't the game. | Can the new direction be played today? |
| 2 | Prototype early; find the core interaction that's fun to repeat; let it tell you what to cut. | What 30-second action is fun on an empty map? |
| 3 | Stop restarting: it brings back the same problems. | Does this direction fix a blocker, or just feel like progress? |
| 4 | Big new ideas go to the next game. | Current build, or "next game" file? |
| 5 | When behind, cut to the essential; limits give purpose. | Which system could go and leave the game more focused? |
| 6 | Rough out the whole thing before refining. | Is there a rough version of every link, start to end? |
| 7 | After an unfinished project, go smaller, not bigger. | Did the last change shrink scope or grow it? |
| 8 | Budget for the final stretch (the last 10% is the last 90%). | Is the boring part budgeted, given job hours? |
| 9 | Use outside deadlines. | What public date would force cuts? |
| 10 | Build to learn; design is closer to drawing than to rules. | Which decisions came from playing? |
| 11 | Randomness needs interacting systems that create unscripted stories. | Do systems interact, or just coexist? |
| 12 | Mix handmade and random; a guaranteed route plus risky detours. | Is every space passable, and does each detour pay? |
| 13 | Ship small, rough and fun first; grow from there. | What's the smallest releasable version? |
| 14 | Loose constraints unify; tie the story together late. | Is the story bible driving the build too early? |
| 15 | Make an honest, coherent game; avoid gimmicks. | Is the core idea real depth or a gimmick? |

# First council review: Hollowmere's Oct 29–30 direction

## Where all six agree (strongest signal)
**Build the core before settling more design.** Each seat gets there from its own angle:
- Yu: docs aren't starting.
- Sakurai and Brevik: find the one verb or click first.
- Fujibayashi: crude sandbox, then a Great Plateau.
- Cheng: solve the most important problem first.
- Street: judge talents in play.

The current stance, "settle the design before building core gameplay", is the one thing every lens pushes back on.

## Other agreements
| Finding | Seats |
|---|---|
| **Three forms triple the feel and balance work.** Pick the form that carries the game, or give each a clear role. | Sakurai, Brevik, Street, Yu |
| **Forms need distinct jobs, but none can be required.** Write a one-line role contract per form. | Street, Fujibayashi, Cheng |
| **Auto-target + "press when ready" risks mashing.** Something must cost timing or positioning. | Sakurai, Fujibayashi |
| **Talent trees are heavy for a solo dev.** 21 points means about 21 behaviours to design and balance; real choices arrive late (tier 4 around level 25). | Street, Yu, Cheng |
| **Difficulty cycles multiply balance work, and re-darkening towns can feel like punishment.** Finish Dusk fully first. | Brevik, Yu, Sakurai |
| **The glow-up should be a payoff moment** (freeze, light sweep, sound) **and pull the player** (visible from afar). | Sakurai, Fujibayashi, Cheng |

## Tensions (trade-offs to choose, not average)
| Tension | Trade-off |
|---|---|
| Fujibayashi's **systemic chemistry** vs Yu's **cut scope** | Interacting systems add depth but cost build time. Option: 3 rules max, only if the core is fun first. |
| Fujibayashi's **free travel** vs Brevik's **slot-machine density** | Long drives starve the loot loop. Option: points of interest every X seconds of driving, with roadside fights and caches. |
| Street's **fewer, bigger talent picks** vs the plan's **Dragonflight trees** | Fewer picks are easier to balance; trees feel richer. Option: themed rows (one pick per tier) for chapter 1. |
| Sakurai's **difficulty as wager** vs Brevik's **D2 difficulty cycles** | Both can coexist: the cycles as acts, a per-run "heat" bet inside them. |

## Top ideas from the council
1. **A two-week core prototype** (Yu, Fujibayashi, Sakurai): one grey-box area, a goal reachable only by mixing forms, three robot types, scrap and part drops, a glow-up on clear. Judge only "is it fun?"
2. **Form switch as a timing move** (Sakurai, Street): switching right as an enemy attack lands gives a bonus (truck = shield ram, roller = dodge-through). It adds depth to the button and rewards a timed switch rather than spam.
3. **Each form opens its own route** (Fujibayashi): roller through vents, bike over gaps, truck through barricades. Every landmark gets 2–3 approaches.
4. **Readable rarity at speed** (Brevik): colored light beams and a sound sting, with rarity tied to stat count. Each boss has a signature part plus guaranteed tokens toward it.
5. **Themed talent rows, one meaningful pick at level 10–12** (Street), with the 2 ability buttons as a garage loadout.
6. **A "Next Game / Later" file** (Yu): Night/Ash cycles, schematics and talent tiers past tier 1 wait there until the loop is fun.
7. **Heat-map logging from day one** (Fujibayashi): log Kai's position every second and check whether players stick to corridors or spread out.
